# Doctoral Content Audit

Use this reference to evaluate academic substance. The local formatting checklist is not evidence that a dissertation satisfies doctoral-level scholarly requirements.

## Claims And Contribution

Identify the central research question, claimed contributions, and scope. Trace each contribution claim to methods, results, and evidence. Distinguish a genuinely new result, a new application or dataset, an engineering integration, and a presentation claim. Do not certify worldwide novelty without an appropriate literature search and corpus.

Check whether the introduction states a precise gap rather than only topic importance, and whether the conclusion answers the stated questions without introducing unsupported claims.

## Methods And Reproducibility

Check whether materials, instruments, sampling, controls, conditions, preprocessing, models, parameters, software, statistical methods, and exclusion criteria are sufficiently specified for the field. Look for unexplained changes between chapters and methods that cannot produce the claimed evidence.

## Results, Discussion, And Uncertainty

Separate observation from interpretation. Check that tables, figures, and equations support the surrounding claims; denominators, units, uncertainty, sample sizes, and comparison baselines should be explicit where relevant. Flag causal language unsupported by the design.

The discussion should compare results with relevant literature, explain agreements and conflicts, state applicability limits, and address plausible alternative explanations. Repetition of results is not a substitute for discussion.

## Whole-Document Coherence

Check consistency of terminology, samples, experimental conditions, symbols, data values, chapter objectives, and conclusion statements across the dissertation. Look for duplicated passages, orphan chapters, promised analyses that never appear, and results not anticipated by the methods.

Evaluate the abstract last against the verified body. It should cover purpose, method, principal results, conclusions, and contribution without claims stronger than the dissertation supports.

## Evidence Boundaries

Classify findings as:

- `internal`: demonstrable from contradictions or omissions within the dissertation;
- `source-dependent`: requires checking cited literature, standards, or datasets;
- `expert-dependent`: requires domain judgment, raw data, experiments, or specialist review.

Never present absence from the supplied text as proof that work was not performed. State what evidence was unavailable and what would resolve the question.
